        Iran 95/100 · Trump 77/100 · Hormuz 12/100
      

      
Nearly everyone agrees on who the actors are. The disagreement is about what happened.
        American coverage leads with the announcement — “Trump says he will hold off on fresh Iran attack in hope
        of quick deal.” Lebanese and Indian coverage lead with the denial — “Iran responds to Trump: we did
        not ask to negotiate,” and “Tehran denies asking US to pause attacks, labels Trump’s claim a new
        lie.” Neither account is false, and the two do not fit together.

      
Naming the strait turns this into a story about a transaction: a reopening traded for something.
        Leaving it out makes it a story about a decision taken and reversed. The split does not run along the lines you would
        guess — two Indian outlets sit on opposite sides of it, and the sharpest denial in the set comes from an English-language
        Nigerian paper. Worth watching over the coming cycles is whether the strait spreads or disappears: if the mention rate
        climbs while nothing new is announced, what changed is the framing, not the event.

A story, not an event

  
Headlines cannot separate “Starmer will resign” from “Starmer has resigned” from “the race to replace
    him” — measured at 0.86 precision for stories against 0.23 for events, and human annotators do no better.

  
Grouping runs in two passes: a broad one that finds themes, then a tighter one inside each theme that separates the stories.

  



Sources counts voices, not articles

  
One per distinct publisher, after collapsing reprints that share a headline word for word. Between a fifth and a third of
    clustered articles are wire copy — six local papers running one agency line are one voice, and counting six would make
    disagreement look smaller than it is.

  



Polities are the unit of comparison

  
Outlets are grouped by the polity they publish from, because that is a fact rather than a judgement about editorial stance.
    Filled marks covered the story; hollow ones stayed silent.

  
A story needs two polities to be measurable — one voice cannot disagree. Real outlets publish in bursts, so silence
    often means “not this hour”, not “not working”.

  
The fixed panel is not chosen yet, so the number here counts the polities that actually carried the story, and the
    globe plots them from the data. Hollow marks are candidate polities that stayed silent — illustrative until the panel exists.

  



Named by, and the omission

  
How many sources put that actor in the headline. The signal is usually what is missing: an outlet reporting deaths
    without naming who caused them has made an editorial choice, and it is legible without anyone judging tone.
